# Learning System
---
A learning system it's necessary to our brain in order to remember relevant information, without debloated with un-used and detailed data. Most of the information that the brain it's getting from outside follow this algorithm.
![[Pasted image 20260830114714.png]]

If **information doesn’t connect to what you already know, or if your brain doesn’t see it as relevant, it gets forgotten almost immediately**. This is why passive learning — just reading notes or listening to lectures without engagement — keeps you trapped in a frustrating cycle of forgetting and re-learning.

>[!info] Simplify
>So in other words, what's relevant for our brain it will be saved in memory and retrived whenever we want, the information it's not important for us, **then bye bye it will be lost**.

It comes in handy a framework that's necessary to internalize the knowledge / information we want to stick in our brain memory.
## PERO system

>[!question] What's the PERO system
>It's a framework that's been design to align the brain to naturally learn what it's important and necessary for our brain. It can be personalized with our approach.

The PERO it's an acronymous that stands for: 

### P - Priming

That's the process that we should **prepare our brain to retrive new information**, where it's a class lesson a long study session or any activity that involves to fetch information for our brain, we **should prepare our brain to get this new knowledge in mind**, Priming is highly effective precisely because it happens early in the learning flow. Get this right, and everything downstream becomes easier.

This is a fast process (5 - 15 minutes) where we analyze what we are going to encounter / study for the session in order to remember the information. This involves the following steps.

- **Scan Headings & Subheadings:** Skim through the chapter or lecture slides to see main themes and visual hierarchy.
    
- **Read Summaries First:** Read the chapter summary, key takeaways, or conclusion before the main text.
    
- **Examine Visuals:** Look at diagrams, bold terms, graphs, and table titles to get visual anchors.
    
- **Build a Rough Concept Map:** Draw a quick, high-level visual outline of 4–7 big categories without details.
    
- **Ask Big-Picture Questions:** Ask yourself, _"What problem is this chapter trying to solve?"_ or _"How does this fit into what I learned last week?"_

### E - Encoding
In this phase your converting the raw information you got from the previous step, into an easy and well structure concept that you mind will remember. For instance we can create analogies on something we read into an existing object.

>[!example]
>For instance let's say we want to explain the concept of arrays, someone who never heard this word would say: "What the heck are you talking about?". But if you are going to explain that's the same concept of a box that contains whatever object we want and then label it with a number. Then it will understand the concept more clearly in his mind.

Also the encoding process involves to: 
- **Relationships & Chunking:** Group related concepts together rather than learning them in isolation.
    
- **Analogies & Connections:** Connect new concepts to things you already know (e.g., comparing blood flow to plumbing).
    
- **Higher-Order Thinking:** Explain _why_ and _how_ things work, not just _what_ they are.

### R - Reference

That's the process of saving the information into a space where you can retrive the [granular data](https://duckduckgo.com/?t=ffab&q=granular+data+what+are+%3F&ia=web#:~:text=Copied-,Granular,targeting) later. That's involve the operation of writing note and offload information into a note taking system app, like this one I'm using. Also separate this information in dedicated piece of data that are interconnected with each other.

### R - Retrival

The process of pulling the information out of the note taking app or to a source of knowledge. In this case your are taking the information out of the memory of you brain so the methods that are used to perform this Retrival it's too.

- **Active Recall:** Testing yourself without looking at notes (quizzes, flashcard and something that reminds you).
    
- **Interleaving:** Practice testing concepts in mixed orders and varying contexts—not just in the order you learned them—so your brain learns when to apply which idea.

### O - Overlearning
It's the ability of practicing a skill beyond it's basics and going deep into a concept that you already grasp the basics. This operation involves: 
- **Automated Recall:** Apply concepts to edge cases, complex problem sets, or tight time limits.
    
- **Efficiency Focus:** Use this step selectively—only apply overlearning to high-priority core skills so you don't burn time unnecessarily.
